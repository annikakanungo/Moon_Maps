How the numbers are made

  • Sun and Earth positions are computed in your browser from an analytic lunar ephemeris (Meeus) plus lunar libration. Checked against a published worked example and against astropy: Moon position within 0.03° and 55 km. It is not a live JPL Horizons query.
  • Terrain is not modelled. The horizon is flat unless you set a horizon mask, a single elevation angle that stands in for surrounding ridges. At the poles real topography decides the result, so treat these figures as a first screening, not a mission analysis.
  • Rules used: the Sun counts as up when any part of its disc clears the horizon; Earth counts as up when its centre does. No atmosphere, no antenna pointing limits.
  • Sanity check: pick Apollo 11. Earth should be in view 100% of the time and the Sun about half.
